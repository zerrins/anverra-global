# Purpose

Define the constitutional principles governing the creation, organization, preservation, discovery, sharing, and evolution of engineering knowledge within the Anverra Engineering Operating System (AEOS).

Knowledge management ensures that engineering knowledge remains available beyond individual conversations, engineers, teams, projects, and organizational changes.

---

# Intent

Engineering knowledge shall be treated as an organizational asset.

The organization shall deliberately preserve knowledge required to:

- Understand systems.
- Operate systems.
- Develop systems.
- Review systems.
- Evolve systems.
- Make future decisions.
- Train engineers.
- Support AI-assisted engineering.

Knowledge should be available when it is needed rather than reconstructed repeatedly.

---

# Problem Statement

Engineering organizations continuously generate knowledge through:

- Design discussions.
- Architecture decisions.
- Code reviews.
- Incidents.
- Experiments.
- Requirements.
- Operational experience.
- Customer feedback.
- Engineering investigations.

When this knowledge remains trapped in:

- Individual memory
- Chat conversations
- Meetings
- Temporary notes
- Private documents

it becomes difficult to discover and eventually disappears.

This creates organizational knowledge debt.

---

# Constitutional Decision

Important engineering knowledge shall be captured in durable, discoverable, authoritative forms.

Knowledge generated during engineering work shall be preserved when it has future value.

Not every conversation requires permanent documentation.

The objective is durable knowledge—not documentation volume.

---

# Rationale

Engineering organizations repeatedly solve similar problems when historical knowledge is inaccessible.

Effective knowledge management reduces:

- Repeated investigation.
- Repeated architectural debates.
- Onboarding time.
- Operational dependency.
- Decision uncertainty.
- AI context reconstruction.

Knowledge management increases organizational engineering memory.

---

# Knowledge Management Philosophy

Some knowledge is temporary.

Some knowledge is durable.
